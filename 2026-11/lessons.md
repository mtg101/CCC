# Introduction to Block Code
A 6-week course for students new to making computers do what they're told. 

Primarily using Scratch https://scratch.mit.edu/ but with a diversion into Makecode https://makecode.microbit.org/ to show the students the BBC MIcro:bit https://microbit.org/get-started/what-is-the-microbit/ and also as a gateway drug into Python :smirk:

Each week will be a discrete project, so we don't need to worry about saving progress week-to-week. Having said that getting the students their own accounts they can save to so they can continue to tinker and explore in their own time is of course ideal.

Each class will have a goal that we hope all the students should achieve pretty easily. There will then be stretch goals for students that achieve the goal early, and that all students can play with in their own time. 

Each class has a handout.md file which can be the base page students start with, as it has the basics for what we're doing and all the links they need. 


# Classes

## Class 1 - November 7th

### Title: We're going to learn how to make computers do what we want them to really do!

### Introduction
It's the first class, and some students might never have used Scratch or anything like it before. So let's give them a quick overview before we throw them into coding, something like...

"We all use apps on computers and phones every day: web browsers, games, messaging apps, calendars, email, music players... 

But in this class we're going to learn how we can make our own apps, and make computers do exactly what WE want them to do!

Today we'll start by getting our laptops to show pictures of music instruments we can click to make them play, and make a clicker game from it. When we show interactive images like this that can move, we call them 'sprites' because they move around the screen on their own like fairies or... sprites!

So we'll have a background picture, then musical instrument sprites over the top of the background that we can click and play them, and score each time we make a noise.

We're going to be following this course to make this: https://projects.raspberrypi.org/en/projects/drum-star

On the first page I can click here to open the Scratch project in a new tab in my browser. 

If you have your own Scratch account already, you can log in and 'remix' this project and same it so you can come back to the project when you log in to Scratch at home. If you don't have an account, ask your parents about it, we sent them details by email about how to get one.

Now see how I can look at the instructions, see what block I need to add, then how I can add them over here in Scratch, and then I test the changes my clicking like this."

(show following and testing at least two steps, so brand new students get the idea of how you Scratch)

"Now try it youself, keep following the instructions one step at a time, and testing it. 

If you get stuck ask for help - and when you ask it helps if you tell us what you're trying to do, and what isn't working, like 'I'm clicking the drum but it doesn't make a noise' rather than just shouting 'it's broken it's broken'."

(knowing how to ask for help effectively is a skill in and of itself)


### Course: https://projects.raspberrypi.org/en/projects/drum-star

### Goal: Complete the basic tutorial:
- basic sprites, clicks, sounds, scores...

### Stretch Goals: 
- add another instrument you can buy
    - what instrument? 
    - what sound does it make?
    - how much will it cost?
- make the tom-toms play both tom-toms
    - make the sound go 'low tom / high tom'
    - show sound effect gfx over the low then high tom as the sounds are made
- show your rockstar when you click their name
    - grab a sprite, or draw your own, for the rockstar
- animate the background
    - do the balloons move?
    - or can you pop them?
    - or maybe it needs a disco ball?

## Class 3 - November 21st

### Title: Pair Programming a Book

### Introduction
"Coding on your own and making Scratch do cool things is great. And if you get a team together for a project some people will code, some do music, some do graphics. 

But for the coders... sometimes pairing up is really powerful! 

Today we're going to try a harder Scratch project, so let's try working in pairs to do the harder project. 

This project is going to make an interactive storybook or comic/manga. You'll tell your story over a few pages pages, so work together to plan out what the story is and what each page will show.

Then start to work out how to code it. How will you turn pages? How will you show the story on each page?

Each time you are going to write some code, agree what you are planning to do, and how you can test it. "We're going to make it so when you click the skeleton, it shakes, and we'll test it by clicking the skeleton to see if it shakes, and clicking not on the skeleton to check it doesn't shake". 

One person will then write the code blocks, then the other person will test it. That makes sure the code you're writing is working as you planned it. Take turns in being the one that writes the code, and the one that tests it."

### Course: https://projects.raspberrypi.org/en/projects/i-made-you-a-book

### Goal: work together to create your story

### Stretch Goals: 
- make it a choose-your-own-adventure!
    - let the read choose what happens next in the story
    - on the next to last page you can ask what the reader wants
    - and then the final page is different depending on how they answer
    - "Does the hero save the Prince, or does she save the dog?"
    - how could you make more choices in the same story?
- what team roles would you like to try?
    - large Scratch projects, like any large coding project, has lots of people working on different things
    - some people focus on the graphics, or the sound, others write the story, and of course many programmers with pair program the code, and then testers will test it all
    - which of those roles sounds interesting to you?
    - try searching online about the different sorts of roles that make up software development teams, or asking family friends what team roles they have with their hobbies or at their work

## Class 6 - December 12th

### Title: Happy Holidays!
"Most people around the world have a celbration in the middle of winter. It's a dark cold time when we all need a good party to cheer us up!

We all know Christmas, but there's Africa's Kwanzaa, Jewish Hanukkah, Hindi Diwali... and they all have one thing in common: getting together with friends and family and local communities to celebrate with light in the dark winter times: lights on Christmas trees, the candles on Jewish Minoras, and Diwali is literally the festival of lights!

So today let's use all we've learned in Scratch to make an animated, and maybe even interactive, winter celebration scene! Make something to show everyone else in the class how you and your friends and family celebrate winter together!

It could be a Christmas tree with animated lights. Or it it presents you can open? What about food..."

### Course: https://scratch.mit.edu/

### Goal: Make an animated winter celebration scene
- start with a blank new project and create a winter celebration scene
- start with a background, then add sprites for people and objects

### Stretch Goals: 
- make the scene interactive
    - can you change the colour of lights by clicking them?
    - can people in the scene say things when you click on them?
- play a winter tune
    - what tune makes you think of winter?
    - how can you play that tune?
- check out other students scenes and learn about how they celebrate winter
    - and talk to your own family about the ways that other people celebrate
    - do you have any special family celebrations that have been passed down?
