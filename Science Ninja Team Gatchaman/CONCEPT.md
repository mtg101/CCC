# The Kids Are Alright
This week a gang of kids in class were hugely disruptive and loud. 

What were they shouting about? Games? Some new meme us adults don't get? Some TikTok video going viral? 

No: they were talking **Googolplexes**.

The previous week they were talking large numbers and fractals and this week one of them had learned about a googolplex and they were all just minds-blown-excited about it.

These kids don't need to be told to be quiet: if that ever actually worked (spoiler: it won't) all you've done is shut down an inquisitive mind. *golf clap*

These kids don't need to be told to do the Scratch lesson: they either already did it in five minutes, or it's too boring for them to bother with.

Then you've got the quiet ones. While the googolplexers are screaming the house down, the quiet ones just turn up every week, ignore the main lessons we're doing (or did it in 5 minutes) and then just do their own thing without asking us for anything, or getting any real benefits from it being a class with experts helping out.

# "There go the kids. I must follow them, for I am their teacher."
- Alexandre Auguste Ledru-Rollin (maybe - if he'd been a teacher not a lawyer)

I don't think the basic classes are suitable for either of these groups of kids. 

I think they all would be better off if we let them find something they want to do, and we just facilitate.

I'm confident that left to their own devices (with enabling adults) something interesting will happen. 

## I think we'd be good at this
Our younger Duke of Edinburgh helpers are our Scratch experts. 

The rest of us teachers/assistants are just experienced coders and developers. AKA old farts who really don't know the pitfalls of Scratch!

I think us old timers would be much more useful helping the kids work out how we solve a real problem, rather than fumbling around trying to find which Scratch block to use...
